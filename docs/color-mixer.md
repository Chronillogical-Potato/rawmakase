# Color mixer and color grading

## Measurement

Camera Raw 18.6 rendered every color mixer slider at ±100, and Saturation and Vibrance at ±50, on nine photos (Fujifilm X100F, Sony A7 II and A7CR, chosen to cover skin, foliage, sky and magenta). Color grading was rendered for shadows, midtones, highlights and global at six hues (Saturation 50) plus Saturation 100 and Luminance ±50, on four photos. `scripts/camera-raw-sweep.py` produces the renders and `scripts/lightroom-scorecard.py` scores them.

## Color mixer, Saturation and Vibrance (engine 4)

Their effect on the default rendering is a hue/saturation/value lookup in linear ProPhoto RGB. The change depends on hue, saturation and brightness. `src/develop/color_mixer.rs` applies measured tables (`color_mixer.bin`: 36 hues × 6 saturations × 6 values, hue shift and log2 saturation/value factors per slider extreme), interpolated trilinearly. Slider position scales the hue shift, the value factor's log and positive saturation's log linearly. Negative saturation scales the saturation factor itself linearly: Camera Raw's response at −25 and −50 is close to linear in the factor, and scaling the log of a strong measured desaturation removed up to twice as much color (Blue −25 on one photo lowered mean saturation by 0.050 instead of Camera Raw's 0.024). Several sliders add their changes.

Checked with Camera Raw 18.6 on three photos (X100F ×2, A7 II) for single bands at −25/−50/−100/+50, all bands at −25/−50/+50, a pair and one real multi-band edit. Mean extra error over each photo's default render, before → after: all bands −50 +0.0019 → −0.0001, all bands −25 +0.0023 → 0.0000, the multi-band edit +0.0018 → +0.0001, Orange −50 +0.0014 → +0.0001; positive sliders and −100 unchanged. The Lightroom X100F scorecard went from 0.0093 to 0.0090 (HSL reference 0.0178 → 0.0113, portrait mix 0.0152 → 0.0120), with no reference worse. With each photo left out of the fit, every slider reproduces Camera Raw to 0.0002–0.0066 MAE, 3–5× closer than leaving the image unchanged. On three of the photos, the extra error over the default render drops from +0.0022 to +0.0003 on average, and for the worst slider (Orange Luminance −100) from +0.0153 to +0.0016. Blue and purple are the least covered bands.

Both the mixer and grading run after the tone curves (basic curves and point curves). With them before the point curve, Lightroom references that combine grading with a faded point curve scored worse (e.g. global blue 0.0206, against 0.0083 after).

## Color grading (engine 4)

Each region's tint is a per-channel gain of linear ProPhoto RGB that depends only on the pixel's luminance. Luminance sliders shift sRGB-encoded ProPhoto values by luminance. `src/develop/color_grade.rs` interpolates the six measured hues and scales saturation linearly. With each photo left out, the tint reproduces Camera Raw to 0.0036 (highlights) – 0.0088 (shadows) MAE, and Luminance ±50 to 0.0036–0.0063. Only Lightroom's default Blending (50) and Balance (0) were measured, so recipes with other values keep the previous operator. On the sweeps, those settings still show the previous operator's +0.015 to +0.034 extra error.

Legacy split-toning records (SplitToning keys without ColorGrade keys) imply Blending 100 in both Camera Raw and RAWmakase, so they also use the previous operator.

**Panel.** As in Lightroom, the panel shows the Shadows, Midtones and Highlights wheels together (3-Way) or one wheel large, Global included. Dragging a wheel's puck sets hue and saturation in whole degrees and percent, as Lightroom stores them; Shift keeps a drag to hue or to saturation, whichever it moves along first, and Cmd (Ctrl elsewhere) moves the puck a tenth as far. A plain click or drag away from the puck moves it under the pointer. Double-clicking a wheel resets its hue and saturation. Every number can be typed. The wheels change the same values the sliders did, so an edit renders identically however it was set. Each drag is one History step named as Lightroom names them ("Shadow Hue", "Highlight Saturation", or "Shadow Hue & Saturation" when the puck moved both ways, which is RAWmakase's own name: no such step was found in a Lightroom catalog to copy).

## Point Color (engine 4)

**Format.** Camera Raw 18.7 stores each swatch as one item of `crs:PointColors` with 19 numbers, and its Variance as the matching item of `crs:ColorVariance`: the sampled color (HSV hue in sixths of a turn, 0–6; saturation; value, linear), the Hue, Saturation and Luminance shifts (−1 to 1), Range (0–1, 0.5 by default), then the hue, saturation and luminance ranges, four points each (outer, inner, inner, outer). Hue points are 0–1 across the swatch's hue window with the swatch at 0.5; saturation points are on HSV saturation; luminance points are on sRGB-encoded value. A sampled swatch gets the hue points 0, ⅓, ⅔, 1 and saturation and luminance points 0.73 and 0.18 either side of its own (clipped to 0–1), as Adobe's shipped presets show. Camera Raw drops a swatch with a hue of 6 or more, a hue range without inner span, or saturation or luminance ranges whose inner points don't hold the sampled color; Lightroom writes an empty selection as 19 values of −1 with a Variance of −50. RAWmakase reads swatches from presets, sidecars and the catalog (as quoted strings or bare numbers; no active swatch was found in a real catalog), drops the ones Camera Raw drops, keeps at most eight, and writes them back in Camera Raw's form. Presets with the Color group write Lightroom's empty selection when the photo has none, so applying them clears swatches.

**Measurement.** The operator was fitted to Camera Raw 18.7 renders of three synthetic DNG charts (no Adobe data): dense grids of hue, saturation and value in linear ProPhoto RGB, with single swatches varying each shift, Range, Variance and every range, swatches at red, yellow, green, blue and magenta, low-saturation and dark swatches, and overlapping pairs (about 140 renders, read in 16-bit ProPhoto). The evidence chose the color space: a Hue shift leaves HSV saturation and value of linear ProPhoto RGB unchanged to 0.001, and Saturation and Luminance shifts leave the hue and the other quantity unchanged, so Camera Raw works in HSV of linear ProPhoto RGB, where the color mixer works, not in an Oklab-like space. The luminance range is on HSV value, not luminance: a blue as bright in value as an orange is selected alike.

**Rendering.** `src/develop/point_color.rs` applies each swatch to linear ProPhoto RGB after the color mixer. A pixel's weight is the product of a hue, a saturation and a luminance weight, each a trapezoid with smoothed ramps (smoothstep raised to fitted powers, rising ramps steeper for saturation and luminance), faded out below saturation 0.058. The hue window is ±0.72 rad at Range 50; Range scales distances from the swatch by 2^(k · (0.5 − Range)) with separate k per dimension below and above 50 (Range 0 narrows saturation most, Range 100 widens it a little). Within the selection, Hue turns the hue by up to 35° at ±100, Saturation multiplies saturation by 1 + 0.9·|x| (divides when negative), Luminance scales value by 2^(1.39·|x| − 0.24·x²), and Variance moves hues away from the swatch (×(1 + Variance)) and scales distances in saturation and encoded value by 2^(0.79·Variance) and 2^(0.21·Variance). Swatches apply in turn, each to the result of the ones before: on a patch selected by three swatches, adding them up overshot Camera Raw by about 0.06 in encoded units, applying them in turn by 0.013. Channels the swatch pushes past white clip there. Black & white renders leave Point Color out, as Camera Raw does. The GPU preview runs the same steps.

On a held-out chart (40 renders, different patches from the fit), the operator reproduces Camera Raw to 0.001–0.008 RMS in encoded sRGB, against 0.010–0.048 without it. On the corpus chart (`pc-*` cases, Camera Raw 18.7), mean ΔE00 before (Point Color left out) → after: Hue +50 2.45 → 0.86, Saturation −80 1.81 → 0.83, Blue Luminance −60 1.75 → 0.95, Variance −80 1.31 → 0.98 and +60 1.18 → 0.97, Range 0 1.22 → 0.99 and Range 100 2.12 → 1.26, a swatch across red 1.96 → 1.05, narrow ranges 1.32 → 0.96, overlapping swatches 2.12 → 0.98, eight swatches 4.43 → 1.45, a near-neutral swatch 1.52 → 1.20, Saturation +100 on a saturated magenta 1.57 → 1.18, with the color mixer and grading 2.72 → 1.89; the default render is 0.91. Luminance +50 on saturated oranges stays at 1.86 → 1.82 (p95 10): Camera Raw clips such colors per channel and turns them towards yellow, while RAWmakase's later gamut compression desaturates them.

## Black & white

**Treatment.** The Basic panel's Treatment switches between Color and Black & White, as in Lightroom; V does the same, and each switch is one History step (Convert to Black & White, Convert to Color). The color mixer's settings and the black & white mix are both kept while the other is in use. Choosing a black & white profile (an XMP look with `ConvertToGrayscale`, such as Adobe Monochrome) converts to Black & White, leaving one for a color profile converts back, and choosing Color while a black & white profile is in use changes to the photo's default profile (Preferences › Raw Defaults). The B&W panel replaces the Color Mixer whenever the photo renders black & white, by its Treatment or by its profile. Copy and Sync carry Treatment with the profile (Treatment & Profile) and the mix with Black & White Mix.

**Auto mix.** As in Lightroom with its preference "Apply auto mix when first converting to black and white" (Preferences › Profiles & Presets, on by default), converting a photo whose mix was never set applies the Auto mix; a mix already set, by hand, by Auto or by an earlier conversion, is kept. A conversion asked for while the photo is still decoding waits for it, unless the edit changes otherwise first; a black & white profile chosen before the photo has decoded converts without the Auto mix. An all-zero mix counts as never set. The B&W panel's **Auto** sets it again as one History step (Black & White Mix: Auto) and is greyed out while the mix is Auto's. Imported edits keep the mixer values Lightroom resolved, with or without `AutoGrayscaleMix`; a preset or sidecar that names Auto without values gets the estimate when the photo is at hand; otherwise the current mix stays, as Auto white balance leaves white balance. Exports write `ConvertToGrayscale` and the mix for a photo that is black & white by its profile too.

How Camera Raw chooses it was measured by asking Camera Raw 18.7 for its Auto mix (a sidecar with `ConvertToGrayscale` and `AutoGrayscaleMix`; Camera Raw stores the mix it resolved) on 146 synthetic DNG scenes of colored stripes: neutral ramps, one or two dominant hues, low and high saturation, dark and bright, two-color scenes and other white balances. Exposure and Saturation do not change it; white balance does (the same scene at 3500 K gets Blue +32 instead of +15). A gray ramp gets Red −9, Orange −19, Yellow −22, Green −27, Aqua −19, Blue +9, Purple +15, Magenta +4, the values Lightroom's catalog shows for real photos converted in it (within 2). The 146 results lie in a three-dimensional family (0.5 slider steps RMS off it), as a mix chosen by red, green and blue weights would. They are predicted by the direction in which the scene's colors vary most: the principal axis of their covariance in linear ProPhoto RGB, as white balance and the profile render them. `src/develop/black_white.rs` measures the mean and covariance of the decoded camera values once per photo (on a grid of about 1024 pixels across, without pixels near clipping), applies the white balance and profile matrix to them, and maps the unit axis to the eight sliders with a linear fit to 122 of the scenes. The others are flat single colors, for which Camera Raw returns a fixed mix of its own; a flat photo gets the gray ramp's mix here. Trained on the rest and tested on 30 held-out scenes, the estimate is off by 1.3 slider steps on average (a constant mix: 5.5), and by at most 10 on any band. On the synthetic chart, which none of the scenes resembles, it gives −8, −18, −23, −27, −17, +12, +18, +6 against Camera Raw's −9, −19, −23, −27, −18, +11, +16, +4. Two-color scenes, where a small change in the colors flips Camera Raw's mix to the opposite end, are predicted worst: up to 30 steps off on a band when left out of the fit.

**Rendering.** The mix changes Oklab lightness by the hue-weighted sum of the band sliders, times the color's chroma, times 1.78 when brightening and 4.37 when darkening, fitted to Camera Raw 18.7 renders of the synthetic chart with single bands at ±60 and +100 and the Auto mix (`bw-*` cases). Neutrals stay as they are. The earlier operator added a quarter of the slider sum regardless of chroma, which also moved grays (the Auto mix darkened the gray ramp by 2.8 ΔE00). Mean ΔE00 per case, before → after: Auto mix 2.41 → 2.30, Red +60 1.99 → 1.26, Orange +100 2.23 → 1.65, Yellow −60 2.71 → 2.30, Green +60 1.44 → 1.13, Blue −60 3.01 → 2.46, Purple +60 1.83 → 1.49; a mix of zero stays at 0.91, as the color render. Camera Raw darkens strongly saturated blues and yellows much further than this (a −60 takes them nearly to black), which leaves p95 ΔE00 of 10–13 on those cases.
